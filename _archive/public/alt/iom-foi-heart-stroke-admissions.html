Isle of Man — Heart & Stroke Hospital Admissions 2016–2022

FOI Reference 2298337 · Response date: 18 March 2022 · Source: Manx Care / Noble's Hospital
Condition: Elevated heart conditions and strokes Coverage: January 2016 – February 2022 Population: ~84,000 Relevance: Phase 3 ACSC gap / Stroke data gap
⚠ Critical data quality issue — do not interpret 2019–2021 at face value.
Manx Care's contracted clinical coders only commenced coding episodes from April 2021. All episodes before April 2021 are subject to retrospective coding. The collapse in admissions from mid-2019 and near-zero figures in 2020 are an artefact of coding lag, not a real clinical change. Any trend analysis using this data must treat 2019 H2 and all of 2020 as unreliable. The FOI requestor explicitly raised this concern, prompting this response.

Chart 2 — Monthly Time Series with Coding Gap Highlighted (Executive Summary Chart)

Monthly Hospital Admissions — Heart & Stroke, Jan 2016 – Feb 2022
Grey shaded region = period where clinical coding was incomplete. Blue line = actual coded figures. Orange dashed = 2016–18 monthly baseline average.
Key insight: The time series makes the coding collapse visible as a line drop — from ~30/month in 2018 to near-zero by mid-2020, then a sharp recovery from April 2021. The baseline reference line (orange dashed) shows what the true underlying admission rate likely was throughout.
Critical caveat: The mid-2019 to March 2021 period must be treated as structurally unreliable. Any analysis of trends, seasonality, or COVID-related demand suppression using this data requires the fully-coded retrospective dataset — which had not been completed at the time of this FOI response (March 2022).

The 2019 annual total (179) looks like a 51% drop from baseline — but most of this is coding lag, not real. 2020's 28 is essentially a data void. 2022 Jan–Feb average of 26/month is tracking slightly below the Jan (~40) and Feb (~31) baseline.
Chart 4 — Monthly Seasonality (Baseline 2016–2018 Average)
Are there predictable peaks and troughs across the year?
Mean monthly admissions from the three reliably-coded years only. Shows the underlying seasonal pattern.
January is the clear peak (mean 40), consistent with winter cardiovascular demand. October is the second peak (mean 35). Summer months (Jun–Sep) are systematically lower — June and July are the lowest. This pattern is consistent with NHS England cardiovascular seasonality.

Chart 5 — 2021 Recovery vs Baseline (Analytical Deep-Dive)

2021 Monthly Actuals vs 2016–2018 Monthly Baseline
The timing of the recovery mirrors exactly when contracted coders started (April 2021). Post-April, 2021 figures track close to — or slightly above — the pre-gap baseline.
Jan–Mar 2021: Still near-zero (1, 7, 17) — retrospective coding had not yet reached these months.
Apr–Dec 2021: Returns to baseline range (25–36/month vs baseline 24–35). No systematic shortfall — suggesting the contracted coders were accurately capturing current admissions once they started.
Implication for Phase 3: This FOI confirms that Manx Care's hospital episode records do contain the raw clinical data for conditions like heart disease and stroke. The ACSC basket (acsc.json gap) is addressable — but requires a formal data extraction request to ensure the retrospective coding for 2019–early 2021 is complete before any trend analysis is attempted.
