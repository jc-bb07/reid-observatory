Pension Funding Gap Explorer

What three separate pension demands — Civil Service (PSPA), the Manx State Pension, and the Pension Supplement — cost over the next 20 years, and what it would take to fund the growth: cut services, raise contributions, delay retirement, or raise revenue. Drag the levers below.

Three different pension streams, two different pots. Civil Service Pension (PSPA — Unified/GUS, Teachers, Police, Judicial, Manual Workers) is paid from General Revenue and directly competes with department budgets today. The Manx State Pension and Pension Supplement are paid from the National Insurance Fund — a separate pot that doesn't touch department budgets unless it runs dry (Treasury's own projection: 2046/47, right at the edge of this 20-year window). The "NI Fund already exhausted" toggle below lets you stress-test what happens if that backstop arrives early.
All figures £m nominal. Base year FY2024-25 (confirmed PSPA/Social Security actuals). Every input below is individually sourced — see the methodology footer.

Sources & method: Civil Service Pension base (£143.3m gross / £95.2m contributions, FY2024-25) and demographic decomposition (2%/yr benefit indexation, 4%/yr salary growth — Hymans Robertson long-term assumptions per PSPA Board minutes 25 May 2020; 3.29%/yr pensioner and 2.18%/yr active headcount CAGR — PSPA_Members actuals 2018-2024) per pension_projection_methodology.md (this project, 10 Jul 2026). State Pension core and Pension Supplement bases from SocialSecurity_Breakdown (LBB Table 6.8g/6.8h actuals FY2018-19 to FY2020-21, retirement-family = 86.3% of total NI-funded benefits ex-COVID, applied to FY2024-25 aggregate £277.7m). Pension Supplement statutory phase-out: no awards to anyone reaching pension age after 5 January 2039 (gov.im); modelled here on its observed −3.6%/yr decline (FY2018-21), which is a conservative (slower) proxy for the true post-2039 acceleration. 65+ population growth (6.01%/yr 2021-26, 2.16%/yr 2026-31, 1.94%/yr 2031-41) derived from IoM population pyramids in demographics-explorer.html (Crown Dependencies Observatory; 2026/31/41 figures are Cabinet-Office-trajectory estimates, not census). Department budgets: Pink Book 2025-26 Table 14B, Total Employee Costs by Department. NI Fund exhaustion year (2046/47): IoM Treasury.

This is a scenario tool for exploring trade-offs, not a forecast or policy recommendation — every lever is a genuine, contestable assumption, deliberately exposed rather than baked in.
