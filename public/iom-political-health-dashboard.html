Sustained deterioration. Performance has fallen from 74.5% in 2021-22 to 59.5% in April 2026 — a 15 percentage-point drop in under five years. Mixed trajectory. Decision-to-treat (31-day) has improved from 79.6% to 85.5%. The 28-day Faster Diagnosis Standard peaked in 2023-24 then fell back, while 62-day treatment has remained stubbornly flat at ~52% — suggesting a persistent capacity constraint in treatment rather than diagnosis.

Long-Term Health Burden — Incapacity Allowance

LTIA Claims by Ailment — 2025
Number of Long-Term Incapacity Allowance claims by ailment category · December 2025 · "Other" includes unclassified conditions
Mental health is the dominant driver. Depression (666), back pain (410), anxiety (250) and stress (159) together account for 1,485 named claims. Cancer (carcinoma, 219 claims) carries the highest average incapacity level at 61%. The "Other" category (3,100) contains unclassified diagnoses — neurological, cardiac, musculoskeletal — and is included for completeness.
Mental Health & Cancer Incapacity — 14-Year Trend
LTIA claims · Depression + Anxiety + Stress combined vs Carcinoma · IoM · 2012–2025
Mental health total (Dep + Anx + Stress)
Cancer (Carcinoma)
Persistent upward pressure from both. Mental health LTIA claims rose 44% from 748 (2012) to 1,075 (2025). Cancer-related claims nearly tripled from 75 to 219 over the same period, driven partly by longer survival extending claim duration. Both trends have continued through the post-pandemic period without reversing.
