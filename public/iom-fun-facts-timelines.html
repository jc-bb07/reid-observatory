Fun facts hiding in the data catalog

Pulled from nine years of "Isle of Man in Numbers" (2017–2025), the 2006 & 2009 Economic Digests, and the 2011 & 2021 Census reports in your data library. Four candidate timelines below — real government numbers, genuinely graphable, and each tells a story beyond the GVA/offshore-repositioning work.

1. Is the Island getting warmer?climate

Ronaldsway's own met records say yes: 2022 was "the warmest and sunniest year since records began in 1947," then 2023 broke it again — the government's own annual report calls out both, back to back. 2024 cooled off again ("disappointing summer"), but the recent run sits clearly above the 30-year (1991–2020) average of 12.9°C.

Source: Statistics Isle of Man / Dept. of Infrastructure, "Meteorological Data" tables, Isle of Man in Numbers 2017–2025 editions (each reports the prior year). 2020's figure in the 2021 edition matches the long-run average exactly and looks like a copy/paste slip in that year's report — worth a footnote if this goes further, or a request to Stats IoM for the real 2020 figure.

2. Crime is trading places with itselfcrime & justice

Violent crime ("Offences Against the Person") has roughly tripled since the mid-1990s — 213 recorded in 1996 vs. 560–712 a year recently — while burglary has collapsed by nearly 90% over the same span. Two crime stories moving in completely opposite directions on the same island.

3. The value of drugs seized went up 12× in a decadecrime & justice

From £171,515 in 2014/15 to £2.1 million in 2023/24 — driven mostly by herbal cannabis and a recent cocaine surge. The average prison population (140 in 2024) is now running 50% over the prison's stated capacity of 92, which the report attributes to longer sentences for drug offences.

Source: Dept. of Home Affairs, "Value of Drugs Seized, by Type," Isle of Man in Numbers 2025 (10-year table already built in, 2014/15–2023/24).

4. From farms and finance in 60 yearseconomy

In 1961, insurance/banking/finance employed 370 people — fewer than agriculture's 1,911. By 2021 finance employed 9,143 (a 24× rise) while agriculture had fallen to 664. The two lines cross around 1981. This is the same structural story your offshore-repositioning work already tells in GVA terms — here it is in headcount, and it reaches back further (1961 vs. the 2007+ GVA series).

Also worth a look

Manx Gaelic, back from the dead: the last native speaker died in 1974, yet the 2011 census counted 1,662 speakers and the 2021 census ~2,000+ — a "dead" language with a growing speaker base, thanks to Manx-medium schooling since 2001.

Douglas is no longer where the growth is: Onchan grew 150% and Ramsey 119% since 1961, while Douglas itself grew just 42% — the population is suburbanising.

Population has quietly started shrinking: 2024/25 recorded 563 births against 897 deaths — a natural change of –334, the kind of cross-over usually associated with much older populations.

Sanctions showed up in the aircraft registry: the 2025 report notes 24 aircraft were deregistered following a directive to exit all business tied to Russia and Belarus after the Ukraine invasion — a geopolitical event visible in a shipping/aviation statistics table.
