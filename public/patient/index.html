A&E — Noble's Hospital
How long are people waiting in A&E?
% seen, treated and out within 4 hours · Noble's Hospital · NHS target: 95%
Getting worse, year on year. In 2021–22, around 3 in 4 patients were seen within 4 hours. By April 2026 it was fewer than 3 in 5. The main cause isn't people arriving — it's medically fit patients unable to leave hospital because community care can't yet receive them. The Gent Review (May 2026) called this "exit block."

🚗 🚑 Drive to Noble's ED
Not everyone stays. Many patients are assessed and redirected — sent home with self-care advice, referred to their GP, or pointed to a pharmacy. If the triage nurse judges your problem non-urgent, that redirect is the right outcome. With Noble's at 90% bed occupancy, it's also genuinely helpful to the people who do need to be there.
Cancer — Waiting times
From GP referral to starting cancer treatment
Isle of Man vs England, 2024–25 · % of patients meeting each standard
The 62-day pathway is the biggest gap. Only about 1 in 2 IoM patients start cancer treatment within 62 days of GP referral, compared with nearly 3 in 4 in England. Once a treatment decision has been made, the 31-day standard (85.5%) is much stronger.

Mental health is the biggest driver. Depression, anxiety and stress together account for 1,075 named claims — and have risen 44% since 2012. Cancer claims have nearly tripled (75 → 219) as more people survive and claim longer. A further 3,100 claims are unclassified.

Islanders live longer — 1.3 years more than the England average. Fewer people per 100,000 also die from conditions where earlier treatment could have saved them: IoM 194 vs England 202 per 100,000. Both are positive signs, but neither replaces data on waiting times, quality of care or patient experience.

Mental health data has not been updated since 2019. We know LTIA claims for depression, anxiety and stress rose 44% between 2012 and 2025. We can't tell you whether services have kept pace, how long you'd wait for an appointment, or what the outcomes are.
What isn't published — and why it matters to you
Stale
Mental health waiting times — not published since 2019. You cannot find out how long you would wait for a first appointment.
Partial
Elective (non-urgent) waiting lists — total backlog is referenced internally but no public breakdown by specialty exists. You can't see how long the queue is for your procedure.
Missing
GP and primary care access — how long to get a GP appointment is not published. In England, 56% of appointments are same-day or next-day. IoM has no equivalent figure.
Missing
Maternity outcomes — no safety, outcome or waiting time data is publicly available for maternity services on the island.
Missing
Stroke care — one data point exists (2024: 36% scanned on time, vs England 52%). Not enough to know whether the service is improving or declining.
