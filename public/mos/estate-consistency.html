Where the 24 agree on specificity, and where they don't

The other pages compare two members at a time. This one looks at all 24 together, theme by theme: where nearly everyone who addressed a theme wrote it up with a similar degree of specificity, and where the concrete rate swings widely from one member to the next. It also finds which members' overall pattern sits closest to, or furthest from, the estate's typical shape.

What "consistent" means here. Consistency is about specificity, not policy content — it does not mean members agree on what to do, only that they wrote about a theme with a similar concrete/process/values mix. A theme can be consistently process-heavy (everyone promising a review) just as easily as consistently concrete.
Only among members who addressed a theme at all — a theme several members skipped is about emphasis and division of labour across 12 different seats, not a finding in itself. This page does not compare Independent and Labour as blocs; party is shown as a badge only.

Consistency by theme

Each bar is the spread of concrete rates among the members who addressed that theme: the dot is the least specific member, the line is the most specific, the tick is the mean. A narrow bar means most members wrote that theme up the same way; a wide one means it varies a lot by member.

Closest to, and furthest from, the estate's typical pattern

Every member's eight-theme concrete-commitment profile, compared by shape (not by how much they wrote in total) to the average profile across all 24. A high score means a member's balance of attention across themes looks like most others'; a low score means it looks distinctive, for better or worse — a specialist focus and a scattergun manifesto can both score low.

Methodology & caveats

Concrete rate. Concrete statements divided by all statements (concrete + process + values) in a theme, for members who addressed that theme at all.

Spread. The bar runs from the lowest to the highest concrete rate among members who addressed the theme; the tick marks the mean. A theme addressed by very few members (see the count on each row) will naturally look more erratic — read the count alongside the spread.

Typical-pattern score. Cosine similarity between a member's vector of concrete-commitment counts across the eight themes and the average vector across all 24, rescaled 0-100 (most similar of the 24 scores 100, least similar scores 0). This compares the shape of attention across themes, not the total amount written.

Source caveats. Joney Faragher (Douglas East) and Sarah Maltby (Douglas South) are scored from the shared Manx Labour Party central manifesto. Damian Ciappelli, Claire Christian, Daniel Davison and Vicki Hewison were read from OCR of scanned leaflets or screenshots. Classification is indicative, not authoritative to the last digit.

What this isn't. Not a measure of policy agreement, quality, or how well any two members would work together — only of how similarly they wrote about each theme.
