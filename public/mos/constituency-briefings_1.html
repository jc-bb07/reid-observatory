Twelve seats, two members each: what did they commit to?

Pick a seat to see its two returned members side by side. Every policy statement in each manifesto was read and classified by theme, and by whether it names a mechanism, number or deadline (Concrete), promises a review or plan (Process), or states a priority with nothing defined behind it (Values).

How to read this. A high Values share is normal for a member protecting negotiating room or one who has not yet held a department, and a high Process share often reflects a genuine commitment to look properly before deciding. A theme a member did not cover is usually a choice about emphasis or a division of labour between the two members, not an omission. This page shows where each named something specific, not who would be the better member.
These briefings are generated directly from the classification. Middle also has a fuller hand-written briefing including a leadership-signal read; the other eleven seats do not yet.

Methodology & caveats

Fixed eight-theme taxonomy. Economy, Housing, Government & finance, Education, Health & care, Safety & migration, Environment & energy, Local issues & transport. Every manifesto is mapped into the same eight regardless of its own headings.

Indicative counts. Classification was done in one pass per constituency and cross-checked for internal consistency, but not double-checked line by line outside Middle. Boundary calls are a little subjective. The pattern across themes is the reliable signal, not any single number.

Source caveats. Joney Faragher (Douglas East) and Sarah Maltby (Douglas South) have no personal manifesto and are both scored from the Manx Labour Party central manifesto, so their figures are identical. Damian Ciappelli, Claire Christian, Daniel Davison and Vicki Hewison were read from OCR of scanned leaflets or screenshots. Vicki Hewison's leaflet says a fuller manifesto will follow. Manifesto length also affects raw counts, so compare concrete rates as well as totals.

What this isn't. Not a fitness-for-office assessment or a prediction of how anyone will govern.
