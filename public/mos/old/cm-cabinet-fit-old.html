If this member were Chief Minister, who fits where?

Pick a hypothetical Chief Minister from the 24 members returned. The tool seats the best-fit Council of Ministers around the table, then lets you exclude people from roles or write your own names in. Fit is built only from what each member's manifesto commits to, theme by theme.

What this is, and isn't. A thinking aid for the Council of Ministers question, not a recommendation and not a judgement of anyone's ability. A member who wrote little on a subject may know it deeply; manifestos are one document each. Speaker and Deputy Speaker are not modelled: they turn on chairing skill and cross-party trust, which manifestos do not evidence.

Department members

Each department also has non-minister members appointed by Tynwald, and the same person can sit on more than one department. The number varies by department, so set it with the + and − buttons. Members are chosen by the same fit measure (role expertise only, without the alignment weight), best fit first. Nobody is offered more than two member seats, and a minister does not sit as a member of their own department.

How fit is worked out

Role expertise. Each role reads a weighted mix of the eight manifesto themes (for example Health & Social Care reads Health 90%, Government 10%). For each theme a member scores 60% on how many concrete commitments they named relative to the most-specific member, and 40% on the share of their statements in that theme that were concrete. The role score is the weighted sum.

Alignment. The similarity between a member's pattern of concrete commitments across the eight themes and the Chief Minister's own, rescaled so the closest of the other members scores 100 and the furthest 0. The slider blends it with role expertise.

Seating. The best set of appointments overall, with each member used once, found with an optimal-assignment method rather than filling roles one at a time. Your pins are fixed first; exclusions are never used.

Cautions. Role-to-theme weights are editorial judgement about what each department does. Joney Faragher and Sarah Maltby share one party manifesto so score identically on expertise. Four members' sources were OCR'd and Vicki Hewison's leaflet says a fuller manifesto is to follow (flagged F below). Counts are indicative; the pattern is the signal. Outgoing office is shown as context only and is not scored.

Department members. Filled department by department in the order shown, each taking the best-fit person still available, so earlier departments get first pick. This greedy order is simpler than the minister seating and can be changed by hand. The number of member seats per department is a setting because it varies and I could not confirm current figures.
