What the Gent review found
Adding beds alone won't fix it. The reviewer explicitly does not recommend increasing bed capacity without first optimising processes, flow, and community pathways.
The primary problems are flow failures: No ED streaming, no functioning Acute Medical Unit, discharge planning starting too late, and flow dropping sharply at weekends.
8–12 medical patients occupy surgical beds every day — the "safari model" — because Medicine is full.
The review raises governance concerns: "questionable consultant job plans, silo-planning and circumvention of normal governance systems" — and warns that Manx Care risks becoming ungovernable.
Community investment is higher priority than beds. Virtual ward, community rehabilitation, and frailty services could reduce daily acute admissions significantly.
The three feedback loops
R1 — ED Spiral (active now): High occupancy → long ED waits → patient deterioration → readmissions → higher occupancy. Self-reinforcing above 85%.
R2 — Elective Trap: Full wards → cancelled planned ops → patients deteriorate at home → emergency readmission → occupies the very bed freed by the cancellation.
B2 — Community Release (currently weak): When community and discharge pathways work, medically fit patients leave promptly → beds free → pressure eases. Currently suppressed by flow failures and DTOCs.
