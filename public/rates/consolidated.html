This page adds up every authority's most recent published year. That mixing is the main caveat, not a footnote: Braddan's row is budget estimates, not audited outturn, so the total result excludes it; years range from 2021-22 (Ramsey, Michael, whose second source PDF is missing or corrupted) to 2024-25 (Douglas, Port Erin, Bride, Garff); and Douglas's and Onchan's rows are General Fund only — each also runs a Housing Revenue Account, self-funded by rents, excluded here exactly as it is on their own pages. Treat the totals below as a reasonable snapshot of scale, not a single audited consolidated account — the Isle of Man's 21 local authorities don't produce one themselves, which is part of why this page exists.

Douglas is roughly half the island's local government

Add up the other twenty and Douglas is still nearly their equal — 48.9% of all rate income raised, 48.7% of all borrowing, 49.8% of all net assets. No other authority comes close: Ramsey, the next-largest, is under a tenth of Douglas's scale on every measure.

Excluding Douglas entirely, the other 20 authorities together raise £16,103,442 in rate income, carry £95,662,506 in borrowing, and hold £291,651,491 in net assets — themselves larger, combined, than most small-country local-government sectors, before Douglas is even added back in.

The joint-board network

Twenty-one separate authorities still lean on four shared regional boards for the services too small to run alone: refuse collection, civic amenity sites and swimming pools, mostly. Three authorities don't just pay in, they host and recover costs from their neighbours — a genuine piece of inter-authority infrastructure hiding inside twenty-one sets of accounts that otherwise look completely independent of each other.

What these totals are not. Not an audited consolidated account — the Department of Infrastructure lays 21 separate Statements of Accounts before Tynwald each year, not one combined set, so nothing here has been through a single audit. Not same-year — Ramsey's and Michael's rows are 2022-23 because a 2023-24 source PDF was either missing from the public archive (Ramsey) or corrupted beyond recovery (Michael); Braddan's row is 2023-24 budget estimates, not outturn. Not full-authority for Douglas and Onchan, whose Housing Revenue Accounts are excluded exactly as on their own pages. Treat this as a snapshot of scale built from the same verified figures as every other page on this site, not a substitute for Tynwald's own published totals.
