The sharper comparison isn't geography, it's official tier

Look at borrowing and the pattern is stark: every Village and Town authority here carries millions; every Parish authority carries thousands or none. That's the real dividing line — villages and towns own harbours, halls, promenades and housing stock that sit on their balance sheets; parishes generally don't. Rate income tells a messier story: Port St Mary's rate income is smaller than Malew's despite being a Village against a Parish, and Braddan (officially a Parish) out-earns every Town except Castletown. Scale and official tier don't always move together — which is exactly why the comparison tool below lets you check any combination yourself rather than trust a single ranking.

Douglas — off the scale entirely

Douglas alone
£15.4m rate income and £91m borrowing — roughly ten times Braddan, the next-biggest authority here. Its £328m council housing stock alone is worth more than every other authority's total net assets combined. Not comparable to anything else on this page; see its own tab on the index page.

Ramsey — second only to Douglas

Ramsey alone
£3.13m rate income, £68.26m net assets, £30.31m borrowing — the largest debt load of any Town Commission on this site. Most of the recent net-asset jump is a £34.96m revaluation gain on its 554-dwelling housing stock, not trading. Also hosts the Northern District Civic Amenity Joint Committee for six other parishes.

Town / Village — infrastructure tier

Peel · Castletown · Port Erin · Port St Mary
£4.5m–£10.8m borrowing each, £17m–£49.5m net assets. Own harbours, halls, libraries, or housing. Peel is the newest addition here and, unusually, its housing account runs a structural surplus rather than needing rate support.

Braddan — the outlier

Braddan alone
Officially a Parish, but £1.4m+ rate income and £18.1m borrowing put it in the infrastructure tier by scale. The clearest sign that official tier and real scale aren't the same thing.

Onchan — Douglas's mirror

Onchan alone
District tier, £2.79m rate income, £66.22m net assets. The only authority on this site whose own accounts independently confirm a joint-board match Douglas's accounts already made — both sides now name the Eastern District Civic Amenity Site and the exact sum paid.

Mid-parish

Malew · Arbory & Rushen
£500k–£800k rate income, both audited outturn, both carrying real but small borrowing. The most directly comparable pair in this set.

Small parish

Marown · Santon
Under £350k rate income, no borrowing, minimal staff. Marown's compliance costs run close to its entire administration budget — worth asking whether Santon, running the same way at a third the size, has found something worth copying.

Northern cluster — confirmed shared services

Ramsey (host) · Andreas · Ballaugh · Bride · Jurby · Lezayre
Ramsey administers the joint civic amenity site on the other six parishes' behalf and recovers £189,240 a year from them; the six themselves run £84k–£199k rate income with no borrowing anywhere. Four of the six name the same regional boards word-for-word in their own accounts.

Western cluster

Peel (host) · Michael · Marown · German · Patrick
Peel hosts the Western Civic Amenity Site and swimming pool boards; Marown, Patrick and Michael all pay in, and Michael also names the same board for its own housing committee. German's own accounts don't use the word "Western", so its match is plausible by geography, not confirmed by wording.

Garff — the crossing point

Garff alone
The only Parish-tier authority on this site carrying real borrowing (£984,935, including a direct Isle of Man Government loan) — closer in that respect to the Village/Town tier than to its own official tier. Also confirmed touching two different joint boards: an Eastern civic amenity site (hosted by Douglas) and, by geography, the Northern cluster to its west.

Michael — two clusters at once

Michael alone
District tier, £267k rate income, no borrowing at either year end. The only authority on this site confirmed on two clusters simultaneously — Western for civic amenity, swimming and housing, Northern for refuse collection.

The Northern and Western clusters group by shared-service board membership, not by budget size — Ramsey (£3.13m) and Jurby (£84k) sit in the same Northern cluster despite being close to forty times apart in rate income, because Ramsey is the one administering the shared site rather than just paying into it. That's a genuinely different — and arguably more useful — way to group authorities than sorting them by scale. Douglas, Garff and Michael each sit outside the tidy groupings on this page for different reasons — one by sheer scale, one by being the sole leveraged Parish, one by touching two clusters at once — which is itself worth noting rather than forcing them into a cluster that doesn't fit.

The full roster

Already collaborating — what the accounts show

Every one of the 21 already pays into at least one shared regional facility. Three don't just pay in — they host: Douglas administers the Eastern District Civic Amenity Site, Ramsey the Northern District Civic Amenity Joint Committee, and Peel the Western Civic Amenity Site and swimming pool. The strongest evidence on this site is now a genuinely mutual confirmation: Onchan's own accounts independently name Douglas and the Eastern site, and give the exact sum paid — the first joint-board finding here confirmed from both the host's side and a payer's own published wording, not just one or the other. Michael is the outlier in a different way: the only authority confirmed touching two clusters at once, Western for civic amenity, swimming and housing, Northern for refuse.

Governance timeliness

Braddan and Castletown are the only two that publish their own accounts where a resident can find them without going through Tynwald — and Castletown's own site goes back to 2010/11, the deepest self-published record of any authority here. Marown's own site has one set, from 2019. None of the other eighteen, including the four newest additions (Ramsey, Peel, Onchan, Michael), publish independently — their figures are public only because the Department lays every local authority's accounts before Tynwald each year, and the Tynwald report's own foreword says a large number of those arrived late for the 2026 deadline. Scale doesn't predict this: Ramsey has the largest balance sheet of any Town Commission here bar Douglas and still doesn't publish its own accounts, and neither Ramsey's nor Michael's second year's source PDF was even available in the archive — Ramsey's most recent published year here is 2022-23, Michael's 2022-23 (a 2023-24 PDF exists for Michael but is corrupted beyond recovery).

What this page is not. It doesn't attempt a cost-per-resident or cost-per-household comparison. An earlier pass at that showed it mixes domestic and non-domestic ratepayers in ways that overstate the picture for authorities with a bigger commercial base, and no published source gives a clean per-property breakdown to fix it. Rate-in-the-pound is the safer comparison and is used above instead.

What would make this genuinely useful

All 21 are built, so the next steps shift from coverage to depth. First, the consolidated accounts page now gives the island-wide totals this page's per-authority rows can't — total rate income, borrowing and net assets, and what share of each is Douglas alone. Second, a genuine cost-per-service comparison (refuse per tonne, not per resident) would sidestep the domestic/non-domestic rate problem entirely, because service volumes are measurable independent of who's on the rate roll — the joint-board network above, now with three confirmed hosts and one two-cluster authority, is exactly the shared-cost data that comparison would need. Third, revisiting Ramsey and Michael once (if) their missing or corrupted second-year PDFs surface would bring both fully onto the same 2023-24 basis as the rest of the site.
