Finance & eGaming unbundled — Isle of Man

The Economic Mix page bundles Banking, Insurance, Other Finance & Business Services and eGaming into one "Finance & eGaming" line. This page takes the bundle apart: which parts of it actually grew after 2007, and how much of the celebrated surge is eGaming rather than finance.

What the unbundling shows. In real terms the bundle grew ~50% from 2007/08 to 2023/24 — but eGaming supplied about two-thirds of that growth, rising from 3.6% to 14.2% of the economy (peak 21.1% in 2017/18). Finance proper grew ~18%, and even that is carried by Insurance (+60%): Banking shrank ~26%, roughly tracking the UK finance sector's own real decline (−19%). The Isle of Man did not out-run the UK at banking; it changed business. Sector detail begins at 2007/08, when the accounts moved to an ESA10 basis and eGaming was first split from ICT.

Reading it. The purple wedge is the story: eGaming was 3.6% of the economy when it was first split out in the accounts; six years later it was rivalling Banking, and at its 2017/18 peak it was larger than any finance sub-sector. The three blue bands — finance proper — are, in total, roughly where they started as a share of the economy, with Banking's decline offset by Insurance. A chart that bundles all four reports the rise of a gambling-and-games industry as growth in "finance".

Why the series starts in 2007/08. Before then the published accounts carry one combined "Insurance, Banking, Finance and Business Services" line and fold eGaming inside ICT — the bundle this page exists to take apart. The 2008/09 edition restated 2007/08 onto the ESA10 basis, and the 2009/10 edition split eGaming out retroactively to 2007/08. Earlier years exist only at the bundled taxonomy (see the 30-year series in the data library).
Read this before the chart. ① The IoM line is the bundle — Banking + Insurance + Other Finance & Business Services + eGaming income over the census headcount of those industries. eGaming books island-scale revenues with a small onshore workforce, so the bundle's £/worker is not what a finance worker produces. No defensible annual eGaming employment series exists (census anchors only: 658 in 2016, 1,086 in 2021), so this page does not attempt eGaming-specific £/worker. ② Employment is census-anchored (1996, 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016, 2021 — dotted verticals) and straight-line interpolated between censuses; year-to-year movement between anchors is arithmetic, not observation.

Output per worker — IoM "Finance & eGaming" vs UK Financial & Insurance (SIC K)
The bundling artefact, quantified. The apparent doubling at 2007/08 is not productivity — it is the ESA10 restatement plus eGaming entering the numerator. From 2008/09 the bundle pulls away from the UK line mostly because eGaming's near-workerless income is divided across a finance-sized workforce that was itself shrinking (census Banking headcount 2,927 → 1,851 over 2011–2021). The gap is real money per counted worker; it is not a like-for-like finance productivity comparison.
Method & sources
Numerator series (shares, levels). IoM National Income Reports, editions 2008/09–2023/24, Table 2 (National Income by Economic Activity at Constant Prices), chained link-by-link with each edition's later vintage where two editions cover the same year. Chain base: 2007/08 "Modernised" (ESA10) column of the 2008/09 edition; eGaming and ICT base from the 2009/10 edition, which introduced the eGaming split retroactively. Full construction and verification record: im-sector-output-real-chained-METHODOLOGY.md in the data library. Independently re-verified against the source PDFs for this page (editions 2009/10, 2013/14, 2018/19, 2023/24 — 100/100 sector-year link checks within 0.005pp, 26 Aug 2026). "Professional Services" here is Legal & Accountancy + Corporate Service Providers + Other Professional Services.

Output per worker. Unchanged from the Economic Mix page: IoM national income at factor cost by industry group over census-interpolated employment; UK Finance SIC K synthesised from ONS DJG5 (output per job index) anchored at £149.3k/job (£170.2bn GVA ÷ 1,140k jobs, 2023); real UK series from ONS GDP(O) chained-volume GVA ÷ JOBS02 jobs (~2023£). The Isle of Man publishes no GVA series — its numerator is income at factor cost, conceptually close to GVA but not GVA.

UK reference share. ONS GDP(O) low-level aggregates (gdplowlevelaggregates2026q1.xlsx, 30 Jun 2026), worksheet 1 (current-price GVA): SIC K ÷ total GVA. Calendar year y is plotted against fiscal year y/y+1.
