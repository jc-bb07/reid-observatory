What this shows: The overall IoM−UK differential (white) is decomposed into two components. Housing & energy differential (red) = IoM CPI Group 04 rate minus UK CPI Division 04 rate. Ex-housing differential (teal) = IoM all-items-ex-housing rate minus UK all-items-ex-housing rate, where ex-housing rates are derived algebraically: rate_exh = (all_items − w × h04) / (1 − w). The two weighted components approximately reconstruct the overall differential (internal consistency check passes to within 0.1pp).

Key finding in 2022: While the headline IoM−UK differential was close to zero (IoM and UK rising together), this masked a large divergence underneath — UK housing/energy was running +20–25pp above IoM housing (UK energy price cap shock hit the ONS basket hard; IoM energy pricing was different), while IoM ex-housing was running +3–4pp above UK ex-housing (supply chain, import costs, island premium). These components nearly cancelled at the headline level.

System dynamics relevance: The ex-housing differential is closer to a "pure price level" island premium — driven by import costs, transport, smaller competitive markets. The housing differential reflects energy policy divergence and (in earlier years) local planning/land supply dynamics. Treating them as separate state variables in a system model is methodologically sound.

Caveats: IoM Group 04 weight fixed at 154.9/1000 (2008 basket). UK Division 04 weights are annual (ONS published weights series CHZU). IoM Group 04 covers housing + water + electricity + gas + other fuels as a bundle — not pure shelter cost. Available from Jan 2014 (when IoM group indices become extractable from Statistics IoM historic datasets). IoM series is CPI (base Jan 2008=100). UK series D7GB is CPI Division 04 annual rate (ONS MM23).

Sources: IoM: Statistics IoM Historic Data Sets Dec 2025 (extracted via Tabula). UK all-items: ONS D7G7. UK Division 04: ONS D7GB. UK weights: ONS CHZU.
