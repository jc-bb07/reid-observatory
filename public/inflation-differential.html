Above zero = island inflation higher than UK. Below zero = island inflation lower than UK. Positive differential means prices are rising faster on the island than on the mainland.
What the differential shows: Each series is island headline inflation minus the selected UK benchmark. A positive value means the island was experiencing faster price rises than the UK in that year; negative means cheaper relative to the UK. The 2022–23 period is particularly striking for Jersey, where the BoE rate cycle fed directly into RPI via mortgage interest — producing a differential of +14pp at peak vs UK RPI, or about +1pp if comparing RPIX to CPIH.

IoM note: RPI (Dec annual %) used 1977–2008; CPI (FY average) from 2009. Series break at 2008/09 means the differential jump there is partly methodological. IoM CPI broadly excludes mortgage interest so is more comparable to UK CPIH or island RPIX measures from 2009.

Sources: IoM Digest of Economic & Social Statistics 2009 (Table 4.2); Statistics IoM CPI Dec 2025 historic datasets; Statistics Jersey Open Data (OGL); States of Guernsey Supplementary Inflation Data Q1 2026; ONS RPI & CPIH.
