What the Gent review found
Adding beds alone won't fix it. The reviewer explicitly does not recommend increasing bed capacity without first optimising processes, flow, and community pathways.
The primary problems are flow failures: No ED streaming, no functioning Acute Medical Unit, discharge planning starting too late, and flow dropping sharply at weekends.
8–12 medical patients occupy surgical beds every day — the "safari model" — because Medicine is full.
The review raises governance concerns: "questionable consultant job plans, silo-planning and circumvention of normal governance systems" — and warns that Manx Care risks becoming ungovernable.
Community investment is higher priority than beds. Virtual ward, community rehabilitation, and frailty services could reduce daily acute admissions significantly.
The three feedback loops
R1 — ED Spiral (active now): High occupancy → long ED waits → patient deterioration → readmissions → higher occupancy. Self-reinforcing above 85%.
R2 — Elective Trap: Full wards → cancelled planned ops → patients deteriorate at home → emergency readmission → occupies the very bed freed by the cancellation.
B2 — Community Release (currently weak): When community and discharge pathways work, medically fit patients leave promptly → beds free → pressure eases. Currently suppressed by flow failures and DTOCs.

R1 — The ED Spiral (reinforcing)
Demand rises → ward beds fill → patients wait longer in ED for a bed → crowded ED means patients deteriorate → they are readmitted in worse condition → occupancy rises further. Active above ~85% occupancy.
R2 — The Elective Trap (reinforcing)
Full wards → planned operations cancelled to free beds → patients waiting for surgery deteriorate at home → they re-present as emergencies → they occupy the very beds freed by the cancellation. Active when elective backlog exceeds ~150.
B1 — Coping Decay (balancing, slow)
Crisis triggers short-term fixes — escalation beds, agency staff — which absorb immediate pressure but drive up costs → financial constraints force future cuts → capacity shrinks → the next crisis hits harder.
B3 — OOH Senior Cover (balancing)
Gent finding: two-thirds of admissions occur out-of-hours when junior staff are on. Junior doctors, risk-averse without senior support, default to admitting patients who could safely be managed otherwise. Better OOH senior cover → fewer unnecessary admissions → lower occupancy. Currently weak: Noble's OOH senior decision support is limited.
B2 — Social Care Release (balancing)
When social care capacity is adequate, medically fit patients are discharged promptly → beds free up → ward occupancy falls → pressure across the whole system eases. Currently suppressed: IoM social care estimated at ~55% of required capacity.

Isolation & Specialist Depth
An island has no land-based mutual aid, and workforce specialisation depth is distinct from OOH timing alone.
Specialist decision depth 35%
% of clinical decisions made with full independent specialist input, at any hour. Distinct from OOH cover — drives LOS and complications directly. Low depth → delayed decisions → longer stays, more transfers abroad.
Air bridge status OPEN
Off-island transfer to Liverpool/Manchester — Noble's only release valve. No land-based neighbour to absorb pressure. When closed, complications stay on-island.
Placeholder, not real data:

When open, the air bridge is assumed to absorb a small daily trickle of the most complex overflow cases — modelled as 1.2% of effective staffed beds per day. This figure is an illustrative estimate, not a measured transfer rate.

Of the daily "complications" generated by low specialist depth (see above), only 30% are assumed tertiary-transfer-appropriate — i.e. genuinely requiring off-island specialist care rather than being manageable on-island. The remaining 70% stay at Noble's regardless of air bridge status.

When closed, this relief drops to zero. Nothing else in the model changes — admissions, LOS and specialist depth behave identically — so the marginal effect you see running the "Storm: air bridge closed" scenario against the baseline is purely the loss of this relief valve.

What's missing: there is currently no real data behind any of these figures — no actual count of urgent off-island transfers, no specialty breakdown, no record of weather-related delays or their clinical impact, and no measured ceiling on transfer capacity when the bridge is operating normally. A data request has been sent to obtain this; until it's back, treat this mechanism as directionally correct but not numerically calibrated.
