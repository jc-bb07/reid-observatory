Charts 1 & 2 — Condition Composition (Mar 2019 – Oct 2021)

Chart 1 — Admissions by Condition Group (Donut)
What types of conditions are driving these admissions?
From the grand totals row of FOI 2074149 Appendix 1 — the only fully reliable aggregate from this dataset. 396 total coded admissions across 32 months.
MI dominates at 43% — this is consistent with UK hospital admission patterns. Hypertension as primary admission diagnosis (18%) is unexpectedly high and clinically significant: these are the preventable admissions the ACSC metric is designed to track.
Chart 2 — Admissions by Condition Group (Ranked Bar)
Which conditions have the highest admission burden?
Horizontal bar allows direct ranking. Acute subendocardial MI (I21.4) is the single largest individual code at 106 admissions — 26.8% of all coded admissions.
Haemorrhagic stroke (21 cases) is 5.3% — at ~30 admissions/year baseline, this suggests roughly 6–7 haemorrhagic strokes per year on IoM. With ~84,000 population this is consistent with UK incidence (~70–80/100,000/year).

Chart 3 — Monthly Admissions by Condition Category (Analytical Deep-Dive)

Monthly coded admissions by condition group — Mar 2019 to Oct 2021
Stacked bar shows both volume and composition. Coding gap months (shaded) show only what was captured — the composition in gap months is likely unrepresentative (most acute cases prioritised). Recovery from April 2021 matches the pre-gap monthly rate. ⚠ Monthly composition is estimated from overall proportions for most months; individual bars show totals reliably, colour splits are indicative.
Pre-gap baseline (Mar–Jun 2019): 23.2 admissions/month — already below the 2016–18 baseline of ~30.5, suggesting retrospective coding was not fully complete even for these early months when this FOI was first answered.
Recovery (Apr–Oct 2021): 30.6 admissions/month — exact match to the 2016–18 baseline. This confirms the recovery period represents the true underlying admission rate, and the contracted coders captured it accurately.
Estimated uncoded episodes (gap period Jul 2019 – Mar 2021, 21 months): ~551 episodes at baseline rate vs 89 coded = ~462 missing. At the observed condition mix this implies roughly ~197 uncoded MI, ~107 uncoded ischaemic stroke, ~84 uncoded hypertension, ~49 uncoded TIA, ~25 uncoded haemorrhagic stroke episodes.

Charts 4 & 5 — ACSC Relevance and Phase 3 Connection

Chart 4 — Hypertension as Primary Admission Diagnosis
How significant is essential hypertension as a hospital admission driver?
Essential hypertension (I10) is a core ACSC condition — admissions for it suggest cases that should have been managed in primary care. 71 coded admissions over 32 months = ~2.2/month at baseline.
If hypertension (18.2%) maintained its proportion across the full 2016–2022 dataset (~2,100 estimated total admissions), that implies roughly 380 hypertension-primary admissions over 6 years — ~63/year — on a population of 84,000. This is an extractable ACSC sub-indicator right now.
Chart 5 — Condition Group vs Phase 3 Dashboard Viability
Which Phase 3 topics does this FOI data directly support?
This appendix confirms Manx Care holds ICD-10 coded data for conditions in both the ACSC basket and the stroke quality topic. Both Phase 3 gaps are extraction gaps, not coding gaps.
The ICD-10 codes in this appendix span both the ACSC composite basket (hypertension, some cardiac conditions) and the stroke care quality topic (I63.x, I61.x, I64, G45.9). A formal data request referencing FOI 2074149 as proof-of-concept would be the recommended next step.
