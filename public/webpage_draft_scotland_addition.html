England & Wales — ONS

  • METRIC (ASMR tab): Age-standardised mortality rate per 100,000, 2013 European Standard Population, ages 10+. Annual single-year figures.
  • METRIC (counts & crude rate tabs): Annual registration counts from ONS bulletin. Crude rate = count ÷ mid-year population × 100,000.
  • COVERAGE: 1981–2024. ICD-9 before 1993; ICD-10 from 1993 (X60–X84 + Y10–Y34).
  • ⚠ SERIES BREAK 26 July 2018: Standard of proof lowered from criminal ("beyond all reasonable doubt") to civil ("balance of probabilities"). Significant upward shift in registered deaths. Pre- and post-2018 series are NOT directly comparable. Chart marks this break.
  • Figures are for year of registration, not year of occurrence. Inquest delays mean registration may lag death by months or years.

Isle of Man — IOM Public Health Directorate

  • COUNTS: Annual registration counts, ICD-10 X60–X84 + Y10–Y34, from IOM Mortality Report 2022 Figure 28 and FOI responses. Year of registration.
  • CRUDE RATE: Calculated as count ÷ 85,000 × 100,000. Population is stable at approximately 85,000.
  • ASMR: 3-year rolling, 2013 European Standard Population, from Figure 29 of IOM Mortality Report 2022 and confirmed FOI responses. Plotted at midpoint year of window.
  • ⚠ SMALL NUMBERS: With ~85,000 population, one death shifts the crude rate by ~1.2/100k. Annual counts range 2–24. Treat single-year figures as highly volatile.
  • ⚠ 2020 SPIKE: Registration-year count was 24; police year-of-death count for 2020 was 19. Inquest delays cause significant registration lag on IOM (12–18 months typical).
  • ASMR values confirmed from FOI: 2014–16=7.8, 2015–17=10.2, 2016–18=10.7 (FOI 1228481); 2020–22=17.8 (Mortality Report 2022 text). Other values read from Figure 29 chart image (±1–2 units).

Guernsey — Public Health Intelligence Unit

  • COUNTS: Annual figures are NOT published due to small numbers. The count series shown here uses 3-year rolling average annual counts from bar charts in Guernsey Mortality Trends 2021 and 2024. These are averages, not individual-year counts.
  • CRUDE RATE: Not shown for Guernsey — cannot derive reliable annual crude rates from 3-year averages.
  • ASMR: 3-year rolling, 2013 European Standard Population. Confirmed: 2013–15=7.0/100k; 2019–21=15.4/100k (Mortality Trends 2021). All other values from published charts.
  • ⚠ 2022–24 ASMR: Two values shown — 9.6/100k (confirmed closed inquests only) and 11.0/100k (maximum if all outstanding inquests recorded as suicide). True value lies between these.
  • Total deaths 2002–2021: 104. Total 2005–2024: 112. 2021 peak: 11 deaths (highest single year in 20 years). 2024: 6 confirmed deaths. Population ~63,000–64,000; includes Alderney.
  • Source: Guernsey Mortality Trends 2021 (Aug 2023) and Mortality Trends 2024 (Oct 2025), Public Health Intelligence Unit, States of Guernsey.

Jersey — Health and Care Jersey / Statistics Jersey

  • COUNTS: Annual counts from Jersey Mortality Statistics reports (Figure 14 in 2020 report; Figure 16 in 2024 report). Definition: intentional self-harm + undetermined intent, consistent with ONS. Year of registration.
  • CRUDE RATE: Calculated as count ÷ ~105,000 × 100,000. Population grew from ~103,000 (2007) to ~107,000 (2023); 105,000 used as approximation throughout.
  • ASMR: 3-year rolling, 2013 ESP, from Jersey Mortality Statistics 2024 Figure 17. Plotted at midpoint year of window.
  • ⚠ OUTSTANDING INQUESTS: Jersey reports note that comprehensive data is only available once inquest process is complete. 2023 figures confirmed; some years may be subject to revision.
  • Age and sex breakdown suppressed under Freedom of Information (Jersey) Law 2011 Art.25 due to small numbers and privacy risk.
  • Source: Jersey Mortality Statistics 2020 and 2024, Health and Care Jersey / Statistics Jersey.

⚖ Comparability across jurisdictions

  • The 3-year rolling ASMR series (all using 2013 ESP) are the most directly comparable metric across IoM, Guernsey, Jersey, and E&W. Even so, differences in coroner practice and inquest timelines mean like-for-like comparison requires caution.
  • Annual counts and crude rates are available for IoM and Jersey — these are honest about volatility. Guernsey publishes only 3-year averages for this cause due to small numbers.
  • E&W ASMR series has a methodological break in 2018 (standard of proof change) that does not apply to the Crown Dependencies, which follow their own coroner systems.
  • All jurisdictions include undetermined intent (Y10–Y34) alongside confirmed suicide (X60–X84), which is standard practice and broadly consistent across series.

Orkney Islands — National Records of Scotland

  • COUNTS: Annual registered probable-suicide counts, 1974–2024, by sex (Persons/Male/Female), from NRS Probable Suicides 2024 publication, Table_4A. Definition: ICD-10 X60–X84 (intentional self-harm) + Y10–Y34 (undetermined intent), consistent with the Crown Dependencies and E&W definition used above.
  • ASMR: 5-year rolling age-standardised mortality rate, 2013 European Standard Population, with 95% confidence intervals, for all windows 2000–2004 through 2020–2024 (plotted at window midpoint year). Source: NRS Table_4B.
  • ⚠ SMALL NUMBERS: Population ~22,000. Annual counts range 0–8 deaths across the full 1974–2024 series. A single death shifts the picture materially — treat single-year counts as illustrative, not a stable trend indicator.
  • ⚠ NOT A CONFIRMED STATISTICAL OUTLIER: in the most recent complete window (2019–2023), Orkney's ASMR was 19.5/100,000 (95% CI 10.7–28.2, n=20 deaths) against a Scotland rate of 14.5/100,000 (95% CI 14.1–15.0). The point estimate is higher, but the confidence interval overlaps Scotland's — by NRS's own significance test this is not statistically significant. See the cross-area note below.

Shetland Islands — National Records of Scotland

  • COUNTS: Annual registered probable-suicide counts, 1974–2024, by sex, from NRS Probable Suicides 2024 publication, Table_4A. Same ICD-10 definition as above.
  • ASMR: 5-year rolling ASMR, 2013 ESP, with 95% CI, 2000–2004 through 2020–2024. Source: NRS Table_4B.
  • ⚠ SMALL NUMBERS: Population ~23,000. Annual counts range 0–8 deaths/year.
  • ⚠ TREND, NOT AN OUTLIER: Shetland's ASMR was elevated in the 2000s (23.9/100,000 for the 2000–2004 window) but has fallen substantially since — 10.4/100,000 (95% CI 4.5–16.3, n=12) for 2019–2023, now below the Scotland average of 14.5. Neither the earlier high nor the current lower figure is statistically significant against Scotland at NRS's own confidence threshold — the confidence interval overlaps in both periods.

Na h-Eileanan Siar (Western Isles) — National Records of Scotland

  • COUNTS: Annual registered probable-suicide counts, 1974–2024, by sex, from NRS Probable Suicides 2024 publication, Table_4A. Same ICD-10 definition as above.
  • ASMR: 5-year rolling ASMR, 2013 ESP, with 95% CI, 2000–2004 through 2020–2024. Source: NRS Table_4B.
  • SOURCE VERIFICATION: figures on this page are taken directly from NRS's own published tables and checked line-by-line, given how sensitive small-area statistics are to transcription error.
  • ⚠ SMALL NUMBERS: Population ~26,000. Annual counts range 0–10 deaths/year.
  • ⚠ NOT A CONFIRMED STATISTICAL OUTLIER: in the most recent window (2019–2023), Na h-Eileanan Siar's ASMR was 18.1/100,000 (95% CI 10.3–25.9, n=22) against Scotland's 14.5/100,000 (95% CI 14.1–15.0). As with Orkney, the point estimate is higher but the confidence interval overlaps Scotland's — not statistically significant by NRS's own test.

⚖ Statistical significance — read before drawing conclusions

  • Using NRS's own method (checking whether an area's 95% confidence interval overlaps Scotland's), none of Orkney, Shetland or Na h-Eileanan Siar is currently a statistically significant outlier from the Scotland average, in the most recent 5-year window (2019–2023) or in most windows checked historically. This is NRS's own published finding, not a reinterpretation.
  • Of all 32 Scottish council areas in the 2019–2023 window, only three were statistically significantly higher than Scotland: Dundee City (21.0/100k), Highland (20.5/100k) and East Ayrshire (19.1/100k) — a large urban authority and two mainland local authorities, none of them island areas. Two areas were significantly lower: City of Edinburgh and East Renfrewshire.
  • NRS's own published commentary highlights "remote small towns" (a Scottish Government Urban/Rural classification applied to small mainland towns as well as islands) as the geography type with the most consistently elevated rate over the past decade — a distinct and better-evidenced unit of analysis than "islands" as such.
  • Wide confidence intervals reflect genuinely small underlying death counts (12–29 deaths per 5-year window per area). This means the data cannot currently rule out a real elevated risk in these three areas — but it equally cannot confirm one. Report the point estimates as just that: point estimates, not proof.
  • Methodological note: Scotland's council-area ASMR uses 5-year rolling windows; the Crown Dependencies above use 3-year rolling windows; England & Wales publishes single-year ASMR. Wider windows smooth out more year-to-year noise, so absolute smoothness should not be read as evidence of a more (or less) stable underlying rate.
